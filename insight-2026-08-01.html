01

Knowledge & Findings

A small, high-quality cycle that answered the two questions a founder actually has to answer: what does it cost, and how is it founded.

Four reports shipped this cycle: a critical reading of Aalberg 2019, org studies of Support Act (Australia) and MusicHelps (New Zealand), and a research report on the 2024 Keio case studies of musician’s focal dystonia — Sata et al. and Honda et al., on which Yamaguchi is a co-author rather than the lead.1 The intellectual gain is real and unusually practical, so it is worth stating precisely before the audit.

The Asia-Pacific pair is complete, and it is the most useful pair in the atlas

Support Act and MusicHelps are the only two full-scale independent music-industry charities this project’s scans have found in Asia-Pacific, and studying them back-to-back produced a budget ladder rather than another country profile. MusicHelps runs a national service on roughly NZ$300–400k a year — one full-time contractor, outsourced 24/7 clinical care through a vendor, a small benevolent fund, and org-to-org regranting.3 Support Act, fifteen years further along, runs on A$4.74M revenue with A$4.66M reserves — about fourteen months of expenses.2 For a Taiwan NPO that converts to a day-one figure of roughly NT$6–8M a year and one full-time person, with a ten-year aspiration an order of magnitude above it. This is the first cycle that answered “what would it cost to start” with an observed number rather than an estimate.

Both confirm floor-versus-room — and answer the obvious Taiwanese objection

Australia has Medicare; New Zealand has universal healthcare. Both floors are stronger than Taiwan’s, and both countries still needed a music-specific room. Universal coverage did not make the music charity redundant in either case. Anyone who says Taiwan has NHI, so what is this for now meets two audited counter-examples rather than an argument.

MusicHelps supplies the sharpest warning: mission dilution is visible in the accounts

In FY2025 its musician-wellbeing spend ran at NZ$45k plus NZ$27k against about NZ$137k in grants to general-population music-therapy projects — roughly two to one. By FY2026 the wellbeing line had fallen to NZ$25k against NZ$134k in grants: roughly five to one against the work the brand and its patrons are famous for, and moving the wrong way.3 Both activities are legitimate; the problem is that the organisation never chose between help musicians and use music to help people, and the spend drifted toward the second while the name promised the first. The lesson transfers directly and uncomfortably: pick one theory of change before naming the organisation.

The 2024 dystonia case studies are the edge case that complicates the thesis rather than confirming it

Two n=1 case studies of a professional drummer with lower-limb focal dystonia describe the signature precisely — bass-drum amplitude down about 40%, synchronisation error −26.2 ± 20.8 ms against −4.1 ± 14.5 ms — while explicitly declining to generalise.5 The correction that matters is conceptual: dystonia arises from maladaptive sensorimotor plasticity, not stress, and 2024 Hannover neuroimaging found no difference in limbic stress reactivity between dystonic and non-dystonic musicians. Calling it stress-caused re-stigmatises an occupational injury. Germany recognises musician’s focal dystonia as Berufskrankheit 2115; Taiwan’s human-factor (人因) occupational-disease list — ten ergonomic conditions — contains no neurological movement disorder at all. This is the one case in the corpus where Taiwan lacks both the floor and the room.

Aalberg 2019 cuts against the project’s own instincts, which is why it earns its place

Across 1,607 Norwegian Musicians’ Union members, personal factors — neuroticism, mastery — dominated psychological distress, while classic work stressors like job control and effort-reward imbalance dropped out once personality entered the model.4 That tempers any assumption that fixing the gig economy is the whole lever. The qualification matters equally: the design is cross-sectional, self-reported neuroticism and distress share method variance, and the muted role of work stressors may be an artifact of the Nordic floor rather than a fact about musicians. An association worth designing around — not a proven intervention target.

The cycle did not file its own best idea. “Rights floor versus services room” is now the analytic backbone of the whole corpus and was tested twice more this month, but it still exists only in report prose — flagged as missing from wiki/concepts/ since the July 5 health check, and still missing.11

Trajectory

The best month the project has had on how work gets done. No movement at all on where it points.

The pace change is real, and it is an improvement

June produced roughly twenty-three country studies in a single weekend; July produced four reports across a month. That is a deliberate deceleration into maintenance cadence, and the quality indicators moved the right way with it. All four July reports declare status: complete (verified DATE) in their header block, meaning the §6 gate-6 status discipline codified on July 5 held at four out of four; the July 12 rules check found new pages born-compliant on citations at thirty-two of thirty-two.15 On the axis of process, this was the strongest cycle to date.

The direction did not move at all

raw/notes/ — the directory the single-tile gate named as its measurable target — is empty, and untouched since April 8. Sixteen weeks.6 There is no Taiwan-named file anywhere in the repository except wiki/concepts/taiwan_opportunity.md, which predates the cycle.

The comparative track is saturating, which removes the last structural reason to keep running it

With Support Act and MusicHelps complete, the only two full-scale independent music charities this project’s scans have found in Asia-Pacific are both studied. Nine policy studies and twenty-plus org studies are live. There is no obvious next country that teaches something the atlas does not already contain; the marginal study has become low-yield. The engine is running well and is running out of road.

So the trajectory is a fork that has already arrived. The track that works is finishing. The track it was supposedly serving has not started. Nothing has been queued to succeed the first, and nothing has been attempted on the second.

03

Direction Reflections

Every precondition the project named for starting has now been retired. What remains is the one act that can be refused.

The preconditions are gone

Capability was answered in June. Evidence saturation was answered this month. The budget question was answered by MusicHelps: roughly NT$6–8M a year and one full-time person.3 The founding-structure question was answered by Support Act: found it through collecting societies and industry bodies rather than benefactors, build the prevention and training line early, treat any government windfall as time-boxed, and publish audited accounts from year one.2 The strongest anticipated objection — Taiwan has national health insurance — was answered twice over by two countries with stronger floors that still needed the room.

What remains is the single act that can be refused

Every task completed this cycle shares one property: no person could decline it. A PDF cannot say no. A charity register cannot leave a message unanswered. The Taiwan tile is the only item on the board that requires contacting someone who might not reply, and it is the only item that has not moved in sixteen weeks. That is not a scheduling problem, and it has stopped being useful to describe it as one.

There is an honest reframe available, and it is not a defeat

The atlas is a real comparative research output — plausibly a strong graduate-application asset, arguably publishable, and more complete than most published surveys of this field. Naming it as the project rather than as groundwork for an NPO would be accurate, and it would let the work be judged by what it is good at. What is no longer available is calling it NPO preparation while raw/notes/ stays empty. Two things are true at once: the research is good, and the stated purpose is not being served.

The question is not “what next” but “which one.” Send one message to one person in Taiwan, or write down that this is a research project. Continuing to schedule both and execute one is the only remaining option that costs something and returns nothing.
