Method

How this list was built

A working corpus, not a systematic review. Three databases searched on 2026-09-12, screened to musicians’ and music-industry workers’ own mental health, occupational health and psychosocial work environment.

Searches. Web of Science Core Collection is the spine (580 records, exported in full to raw/notes/WoS_export_musicians_2021-2026_2026-09-12.txt); PubMed added 346 deduplicated records, Scopus 789.

The exact Web of Science query

TS=((musician* OR "music industry" OR "music workers") AND ("mental health" OR depression OR anxiety OR wellbeing OR "psychological distress" OR burnout OR suicide OR "occupational health" OR psychosocial OR "working conditions" OR precarious OR "occupational stress")) NOT TS=("music therapy" OR "music listening" OR dementia OR stroke OR parkinson) AND PY=(2021-2026)

580 → 186. A keyword rule kept 267 of the 580 titles; merging the PubMed and Scopus hits with the works cited in the companion overview, then removing 103 records by hand, leaves 186 rows — 174 in the Web of Science export, 12 not. The rule drops music therapy and music-as-intervention, the physical-health literature, music cognition and pedagogy; it is blunt on purpose, which is why the hand removals exist. Those follow the two SPIDER axes used by this field’s spine scoping review: the sample must be musicians or music-industry workers, the phenomenon their own health or work. Two further rules apply: student- and music-teacher-sample studies are out (the companion overview’s scope has always been professionals and industry workers; mixed samples that also contain professionals stay, tagged Mixed, so a student sub-group can still be present there), and only peer-reviewed journal articles are in — four book chapters were removed on Crossref’s record type. Every removal is listed below with its reason.

Two tag columns. Study type is the article’s form; population is the sample it studies. Both are rule-assigned from title and abstract, except 13 held records whose abstract carried no design evidence — those were read from the PDF’s method section and are marked ● read from full text. Where the evidence is absent the cell reads Undetermined (50 rows) or Unstated (23) rather than guessing. There is deliberately no topic column: one topic label cannot be assigned from a title and abstract without discarding most of what a record is about.

Job titles are a sub-tag, and mostly empty — which is the finding. Where a record names the role it studies, that role is tagged under the population (a record can carry more than one). 142 of 186 name none. The counts below are not a sampling artefact: across the whole corpus the words engineer, crew, roadie, technician, composer and songwriter appear in no title or abstract at all, and producer in one. Composers / songwriters, recording, mixing and production engineers, live crew and backstage technicians, managers, labels and agents are represented by zero records.

Tiers and abstracts. Cited — 19 rows whose PDF is held and which the overview cites. Held — 28 whose PDF is held but which it does not cite; held means the file is there, not that it was read. Screened — 139 with no PDF: not read, not appraised. 150 rows carry the authors’ own abstract verbatim (124 from Crossref, 22 from PubMed, 4 from the PDF held here); nothing in that column is written, paraphrased or translated by this project, which is why a few appear in the article’s original language.

Provenance

Where every field comes from

  • Titles, authors, journals, years — the Web of Science export of 2026-09-12, completed from the Crossref REST API. The export’s abstract and times-cited columns came back empty, so neither is used.
  • Abstracts — Crossref publisher deposits, PubMed, or the PDF held here. Stated per row.
  • DOIs and open-access locations — Crossref, the NCBI ID Converter and Unpaywall.
  • Study type, population, the screening rule and the tiers — this project’s own work, described above; not from any database.
  • Not searched — PsycInfo, Chinese-language indexes (華藝, TCI-HSS), grey literature.
