Method

How this list was built

A working corpus, not a systematic review. Three databases searched on 2026-09-12, screened to musicians’ and music-industry workers’ own mental health, occupational health and psychosocial work environment.

Searches. Web of Science Core Collection is the spine, searched in two arms — 580 records for mental health and the psychosocial work environment (raw/notes/WoS_export_musicians_2021-2026_2026-09-12.txt) and 391 for occupational disease and injury (same folder, …_occdisease_…); PubMed added 346 deduplicated records, Scopus 789.

The exact Web of Science query

TS=((musician* OR "music industry" OR "music workers") AND ("mental health" OR depression OR anxiety OR wellbeing OR "psychological distress" OR burnout OR suicide OR "occupational health" OR psychosocial OR "working conditions" OR precarious OR "occupational stress")) NOT TS=("music therapy" OR "music listening" OR dementia OR stroke OR parkinson) AND PY=(2021-2026)

And the occupational disease and injury arm, added 2026-09-12 (391 records, raw/notes/WoS_export_musicians_occdisease_2021-2026_2026-09-12.txt; parkinson was dropped from the NOT clause because it was removing musicians’ dystonia papers):

TS=((musician* OR "music industry" OR "music workers") AND ("occupational disease" OR "occupational injury" OR "occupational injuries" OR "playing-related" OR "performance-related pain" OR musculoskeletal OR dystonia OR "overuse injury" OR "overuse injuries" OR "hearing loss" OR tinnitus OR "noise-induced" OR "work-related injury" OR "work-related injuries")) NOT TS=("music therapy" OR "music listening" OR dementia OR stroke) AND PY=(2021-2026)

Two searches, 261 rows. The first arm (mental health, psychosocial and occupational-health terms) returned 580 records; a keyword rule kept 267 of them, and merging the PubMed and Scopus hits with the works cited in the companion overview, then removing records by hand, left 186 rows. That rule also discarded the whole physical and occupational-disease literature — 52 records, among them papers in Annals of Work Exposures and Health, Journal of Occupational Rehabilitation and Applied Ergonomics — without listing them, which contradicted this corpus’s own scope. The second arm, run 2026-09-12, searches that literature directly: 391 records, 92 of them already retrieved by the first arm; a rule screen left 93 whose sample is professionals or industry workers; each of those abstracts was then read, 18 were removed and 75 added. Total 261 rows — 249 in one of the two Web of Science exports, 12 not, with 121 hand removals listed below. What the keyword rules still drop is music therapy and music-as-intervention, music cognition and pedagogy. Screening follows the two SPIDER axes used by this field’s spine scoping review: the sample must be musicians or music-industry workers, the phenomenon their own mental health, occupational health or psychosocial work environment. Two further rules apply: student- and music-teacher-sample studies are out (the scope has always been professionals and industry workers; mixed samples that also contain professionals stay, tagged Mixed, so a student sub-group can still be present there), and only peer-reviewed journal articles are in — four book chapters were removed on Crossref’s record type. Every removal is listed below with its reason.

Two tag columns. Study type is the article’s form; population is the sample it studies. Both are rule-assigned from title and abstract, except 14 held records whose abstract carried no design evidence — those were read from the PDF’s method section and are marked ● read from full text. Where the evidence is absent the cell reads Undetermined (59 rows) or Unstated (23) rather than guessing. There is deliberately no topic column: one topic label cannot be assigned from a title and abstract without discarding most of what a record is about.

Job titles are a sub-tag, and mostly empty — which is the finding. Where a record names the role it studies, that role is tagged under the population (a record can carry more than one). 180 of 261 name none. The counts below are not a sampling artefact: across the whole corpus crew, roadie, composer, songwriter appear in no title or abstract at all, and engineer in 2, technician in 1, producer in 1. Composers / songwriters, live crew and backstage technicians, managers, labels and agents are represented by zero records.

Tiers and abstracts. Cited — 19 rows whose PDF is held and which the overview cites. Held — 28 whose PDF is held but which it does not cite; held means the file is there, not that it was read. Screened — 214 with no PDF: not read, not appraised. 224 rows carry the authors’ own abstract verbatim (124 from Crossref, 74 from the Web of Science exports, 22 from PubMed, 4 from the PDF held here); nothing in that column is written, paraphrased or translated by this project, which is why a few appear in the article’s original language.

Provenance

Where every field comes from

  • Titles, authors, journals, years — the Web of Science export of 2026-09-12, completed from the Crossref REST API. The export’s abstract and times-cited columns came back empty, so neither is used.
  • Abstracts — Crossref publisher deposits, PubMed, or the PDF held here. Stated per row.
  • DOIs and open-access locations — Crossref, the NCBI ID Converter and Unpaywall.
  • Study type, population, the screening rule and the tiers — this project’s own work, described above; not from any database.
  • Not searched — PsycInfo, Chinese-language indexes (華藝, TCI-HSS), grey literature.
